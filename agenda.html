Current work

Costs and context in implementation

My dissertation examines how implementation strategies consume resources, create organizational demands, and interact with state policy.

01

Comparative costs of implementation strategies

Using time-driven activity-based costing across 48 primary care practices in the STUN-II trial, I compare practice facilitation alone and in combination with learning collaboratives, performance incentives, or both. The analysis identifies major cost drivers and examines uncertainty in personnel time, travel, overhead, and incentive payments.

Economic evaluationPragmatic trialsPrimary care
02

Organizational burden beyond measured cost

Qualitative interviews with clinical and non-clinical staff examine opportunity costs, administrative burden, productivity effects, and changes in capacity for future implementation. These findings are integrated with trial costs and effects in a mixed-methods cost-effectiveness analysis.

Mixed methodsQualitative researchImplementation capacity
03

The policy architecture of MOUD implementation

A best-fit framework synthesis translates evidence on state policies affecting medications for opioid use disorder into a codebook of measurable implementation-relevant features. The aim is to make policy design comparable across states and usable in implementation research.

Policy mappingFramework synthesisMOUD
04

I plan to adapt my policy-mapping methods to advance-directive registries and electronic POLST systems, then study how organizations maintain and use those records and what that work costs. My research on medical assistance in dying will also examine what oversight systems can observe across jurisdictions. These projects ask how policy and organizational practice can help patients’ preferences inform care.
