Research

How policy becomes care

I study which policy provisions help organizations put care into practice, what resources that requires, and whether the results reflect patients’ goals.

Core questions

Policy design

Which rules assign responsibility and resources for maintaining and sharing patients’ care preferences?

Organizational practice and costs

How do organizations put those rules into practice, and what staff time and other resources does that require?

Oversight and learning

What can monitoring systems reveal about end-of-life care, and where are the gaps in what they observe?

Research program

My research connects health policy, implementation science, and economic evaluation to understand how policies translate into care. I examine how policy design supports or constrains implementation, what organizations need to put policies into practice, and how those efforts affect care delivery and healthcare spending.

My dissertation develops these questions through substance-use care in primary care, combining implementation costing, research on organizational demands, and methods for identifying and measuring policy features. My work in advance care planning and assisted-dying oversight provides a complementary foundation in end-of-life care.

Building on these areas, I am developing a program that combines policy measurement with evaluations of implementation and its clinical and economic consequences. I am particularly interested in opportunities to reduce administrative burden, improve the use of healthcare resources, and strengthen the information available to evaluate whether policies achieve their intended goals.

Methods

  • Cost-effectiveness and decision analysis
  • Mixed-methods implementation research
  • Policy mapping and regulatory analysis
  • Evidence synthesis and meta-analysis
  • Medicare claims analysis
  • Qualitative methods
